The 1.3 multiplier is a judgement informed by descriptive evidence, not an estimated causal premium. Among transfers between clubs of the five leagues dated 1 June 2021 to 31 May 2025, for players aged 26 or under with recorded fee and value of at least 5 million euros (rows missing either drop out), English players sold by Premier League clubs went for a median of 1.27 times the value stored with the transfer (50 transfers), against 1.06 for all others (493); non-English players sold by Premier League clubs went for 1.22 (95), so nationality is not separated from the selling league. A re-run on 19 November 2026 gave the same figures. The rival-club mark-ups are team judgement.

\subsection{Construction of the quality proxy}\label{app:score}

For player $p$ and season $s$, $w_s$ is 0.3 for 2023/24 and 0.7 for 2024/25, divided by the sum of the weights of the seasons the player has; $m_s$ is league minutes for all clubs, $M_s$ the league matches of the main club, $g_s + a_s$ goals plus assists, $n_s$ starts, $c_s$ goals conceded in those starts and $P_s$ the main club's league points per match. Then
\begin{align*}
\text{playing time} &= \textstyle\sum_s w_s m_s / (90 M_s), & \text{team level} &= \textstyle\sum_s w_s P_s, \\
\text{attacking output} &= 90 \textstyle\sum_s w_s (g_s + a_s) \big/ \textstyle\sum_s w_s m_s, & \text{defensive output} &= \textstyle\sum_s w_s c_s \big/ \textstyle\sum_s w_s n_s,
\end{align*}
and market value is the latest valuation on or before the cutoff. Each component becomes a percentile by \eqref{eq:percentile}, with defensive output multiplied by $-1$ first. Output is ranked only among players with at least 900 weighted minutes (ten weighted starts for CB and GK); the others receive 50. The score is $q_{pg} = \sum_c \theta_{gc} \pi_{pgc}$. Tables~\ref{tab:scoreseasons} and~\ref{tab:scoreparts} work through one attacking and one defensive example. Percentiles are stored to one decimal place, so the rounded contributions can differ from the stored score by 0.01.
